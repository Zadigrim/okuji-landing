What okuji is

A passport for the places that mattered.

Not check-ins. Not points. A quiet record of where you actually went and what you noticed there — verified, kept, and yours.

01

Collect

Earn a stamp only when you're actually there — GPS-verified at the real place, like a hand stamp in a paper passport.

02

Remember

Keep a private journal of what you saw and thought — notes that stay attached to the place and the moment.

03

Explore

Follow passports made by people and places worth trusting — trails of real stops, not an algorithm's idea of nearby.

04

Keep

A finished passport is yours to keep — the stamps, the notes, the day it happened — not a feed that scrolls away.

A stamp is a moment, dated.

Each stamp is placed with a press, the way a hand stamp meets paper — and the passport keeps a faint trace of who pressed it.

What a camera roll can't hold.

A voice note, what someone said, what surprised you — and you can return to add to any entry years later.

It respects your time.

Stamp, note, done — usually under ninety seconds, then the phone goes away. No streaks, no nudges, no reason to stay.

Every direction counts.

The neighborhood park earns the same stamp as the far-off place. Attention is the only requirement.

The promise

Built on attention, not surveillance.

okuji makes money from people who value it — never from watching them. The difference is the whole point.

No background location

okuji only checks where you are the moment you choose to stamp. Never in the background, never while you sleep.

No ads

No banners, no sponsored stops, no feed engineered to keep you scrolling. The product is the passport.

No data sale

Your places and notes are never sold, brokered, or handed to advertisers. There is no second customer.

Your journal is yours

What you write stays private to you by default — exportable, deletable, never mined. You hold the key.

No background location · no ads · no data sale · your journal is yours.

“Each day is a journey, and the journey itself home.”
Bashō · Oku no Hosomichi · 奥の細道

The name comes from Bashō's Oku no Hosomichi — “the narrow road to the deep north.” okuji is for the going, and for remembering it.

For institutions

Make a passport for your place.

Teachers, librarians, rangers, museum educators, and civic partners use okuji to turn real places into something worth completing.

  • Libraries — summer reading and branch-to-branch challenges, run as a stamp passport.
  • Teachers & schools — field-trip passports and place-based learning, with a note from a ranger or docent kept in a child's passport.
  • Parks & rangers — trail passports and verified-presence visitor engagement, with no paper to print or fake.
  • Museums — guided exhibit circuits with verified completion.
  • Towns & civic partners — downtown “shop & explore” passports and waterfront trails, with real foot-traffic data.
